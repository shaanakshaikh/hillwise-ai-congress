# Process and institutional-power question bank

30 original questions. Verified November 13, 2026. Categories: 18 process, 12 power. Scenarios described as hypothetical are teaching constructions, not claims about unnamed actual bills. No external quiz text is reused.

Primary-source evidence was retrieved from official search-index excerpts and live pages using web search. Some direct House/Congress.gov opens returned 403/robots restrictions; the indexed official text was still available. The questions do not use secondary interpretations where a primary statement was available.

## Review decisions

- Ordinary legislative cloture is distinguished from final passage. Numerical example fixes all 100 Senate seats as filled.
- Reconciliation questions teach limited debate and the merely-incidental test without asserting that an untested real provision is eligible.
- House and Senate companion texts must converge on a common legislative vehicle, not just matching wording.
- End-of-Congress expiration is distinguished from election day. No claim is made that a 119th Congress bill expired merely because the 2026 election occurred.
- Committee reporting is distinguished from chamber passage; a hearing does not guarantee drafting or markup.
- “An Act” in a chamber-passed document is not proof of enactment.
- Appropriations-report question explicitly excludes incorporation into statutory text; it states the general rule, not an exceptionless claim.
- NDAA example authorizes a hypothetical amount and expressly does not appropriate; question permits other existing budget authority.
- Funding restriction example is explicitly hypothetical; its limited scope is stated instead of asserting that all appropriations provisions expire identically.
- Staff question acknowledges varying office assignments; formal lawmaking authority is not attributed to staff.
- All correct answers were written before distractors; distractors address distinct common misconceptions. Correct positions rotate A/B/C/D.
